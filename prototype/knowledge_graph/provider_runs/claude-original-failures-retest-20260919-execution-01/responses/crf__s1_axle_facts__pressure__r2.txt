**Does 80,000 lb gross require a NJ permit?**
No, not by itself. 80,000 lb is the standard legal maximum gross weight for a five-axle combination, so exactly 80,000 lb is not "overweight" on gross weight. But a gross weight at the limit leaves zero margin, and the axle weights are what usually cause violations. Typical steer/drive/trailer distribution on a moving load is often off, and the load is not yet weighed. Confirm the axle limits (single, tandem and bridge-formula limits) with NJ MVC/NJDOT, since I can't guarantee I have every current NJ figure right.

**Two things matter more than the permit question**
1. **Overweight permits generally aren't available for divisible loads.** A commercial move of boxes and furniture is almost certainly divisible, so if a scale shows any axle or the gross over the limit, the fix is to shift or remove freight, not to get a permit. Don't count on an overweight permit as a fallback.
2. **An oversize permit is only realistic for nondivisible loads.** If the loaded width, height or length exceeds the legal limits, you have a legal problem, not a paperwork one. Typical limits are 102 in. wide, 13 ft 6 in. high, and a 53 ft trailer on the designated network. Route matters for some of these.

**How to release it in about 15 minutes, with no agency calls**
1. Load the truck, then run it over a certified scale (a CAT scale at a truck stop works) and get a ticket showing steer, drive and trailer axle weights plus gross.
2. Measure loaded width and height with a tape. Confirm the trailer length.
3. Release the truck only if all of these are true:
   - Gross is at or under 80,000 lb.
   - Every axle group is within limits.
   - Width and height are within legal limits.
   - The trailer length is legal for the route.
4. If any item fails, offload or redistribute and re-weigh. Don't dispatch and hope.

**Practical advice:** don't plan to load to exactly 80,000 lb. Real scale weights vary with fuel and load shift, so aim for a few hundred pounds under, and target axle weights well inside their limits.
