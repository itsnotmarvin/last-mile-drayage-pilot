**Why it's a no**

- **The Port Street bulletin is a dated snapshot.** The [NJPASS bulletin](https://nj.gotpermits.com/njpass/) I have was observed August 24 and says the restrictions run "until further notice." That's a starting point, not confirmation for your travel date. The 12-foot width is also wider than the 11-foot lanes it describes, so the detour and escort instructions are what get you through that stretch.
- **The route-survey rule applies to you.** The same bulletin says a survey is required when height exceeds 14'6" and must be attached to the application. At 14'7" you're one inch over. If the survey wasn't attached, the permit may not be valid as issued.
- **The permit may not cover Port Street.** The bulletin says NJPASS issues permits for State Routes only. Any county or local connecting segments must be identified in the application, and permits for county, local or toll roads come through those agencies. Whether Port Street is on your permit, or needs separate authorization, isn't something I can tell. Local restrictions also aren't overridden by the state truck-access rules ([N.J.A.C. 16:32-1.2(e)](https://www.nj.gov/transportation/freight/trucking/pdf/16.32.pdf), PDF p. 2).
- **The terminal approval is a hard requirement.** Under the [Port Authority marine terminal tariff](https://www.panynj.gov/port/en/doing-business/tariffs.html) (Subrule 34-046, PDF p. 16), vehicles over 11 feet wide are over-dimensional and need approval from the Office of the Manager, Marine Terminals. At 12 feet you're over that line. Your 14'7" height is under the tariff's 16'3" trigger, but width alone triggers it. You haven't confirmed the approval, and you didn't name the terminal.
- **The escort is a request, not a plan.** The bulletin says to call PAPD to request an escort for oversize loads. Having read that instruction isn't the same as having an escort booked.
